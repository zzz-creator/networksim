Networks / VLANs

A VLAN is shared across the network. The gateway is chosen automatically — the closest L3 device (L3 switch / firewall) connected to its hosts. Inter-VLAN traffic is thus routed on the L3 switch and its ACL is applied.
The network lives on its own – background traffic (ARP, DHCP, broadcast) runs all the time. The simulation adds user traffic on top: web, mail, transfers.

Welcome to networksimv0.7

v0.7 · What's new›
  • New courses: DHCP – how a device comes by an address at all, from the four DORA messages through lease time and reservations to a drained range and a rogue server on the network. And splitting a network into subnets – how many addresses you really need, where a subnet starts and ends, and what breaks your own plan.
  • Hands-on tasks where there used to be only text: split a flat network into VLANs, build a trunk between switches, add a default route, look inside the MAC table – and walk a network with no rules at all to see just how much gets through.
  • TLS inspection on the IDS/IPS sensor: the sensor cannot see into encrypted traffic until you turn it on. And the cost is right there – it can read the content only because every client has to trust the company authority.
  • 802.1X on the socket: the port opens only after authentication against RADIUS. A visitor laptop has nothing to identify itself with and stays out – and when RADIUS goes down, company machines cannot authenticate either. You get to try both sides of that coin.
  • BPDU guard: an access port shuts down when a switch shows up behind it, so a foreign box under a desk cannot take over as root bridge and reroute the traffic.
  • Voice priority (QoS) on a congested link: calls stop stuttering, but file copies get no faster – priority is not magic, it is the order in the queue. The test result tells you so.

networksim is a computer-network simulator with a built-in learning platform. In the courses (from the basics to advanced level) you’ll understand how networks work — visual, step by step and jargon-free. In the simulator you then build and test your own network: where traffic flows, how far an attacker reaches, and what survives a failure. Not sure where to start? Take the course.
The app is in active development – something broken or missing? Let me know (bugs, ideas, what's missing).
The app works on phones, but it's cramped. For a smoother experience I recommend a computer.
Prefer to poke around the simulator?
  1. Drag devices from the left palette onto the canvas (switches, firewall, hosts…).
  2. Draw links by dragging from the "+" port on a node's edge. Double-click = device settings.
  3. On the right, pick a source and destination and run a path test (the basic test is a ping). Blast-radius and the resilience (N-1) test are under the "Advanced: analysis & simulation" section; start the live traffic simulation with the button below it.
  4. Fastest start: load a ready-made network from "Examples ▾" at the top and explore how it works.
Everything runs in your browser — no sign-up. Topologies are stored locally and you can export or import them as a file anytime.
